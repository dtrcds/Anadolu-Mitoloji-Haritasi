# Anatolian Mythology Map

An interactive map exploring the mythological figures and stories associated with the seven geographical regions of Türkiye.

**Project Type:** University Localization Course Project  
**Institution:** Bolu Abant İzzet Baysal University  
**Year:** 2026

Developed as part of a university-level localization course, this collaborative project combines web design, front-end development, visual storytelling, and multilingual localization to introduce users to Anatolian mythology.

**[View the Live Website](https://dtrcds.github.io/Anadolu-Mitoloji-Haritasi/)** · **[Explore the Source Code](https://github.com/dtrcds/Anadolu-Mitoloji-Haritasi)**

## About the Project

The Anatolian Mythology Map is a collaborative localization project developed as part of a university course. It combines web design, interactive elements, visual storytelling, and multilingual content to introduce users to Anatolian mythology through a regional map of Türkiye.

Users can explore the country's seven geographical regions and discover mythological content through an interactive web experience.

The project brings together cultural research, visual design, front-end development, and localization to make mythology more accessible to a wider audience.

## Features

- **Interactive Map:** Explore Türkiye through its seven geographical regions.
- **Regional Content:** Navigate to dedicated pages for the Aegean, Marmara, Central Anatolia, Black Sea, Mediterranean, Eastern Anatolia, and Southeastern Anatolia regions.
- **Interactive Elements:** Discover content through map interactions and visual effects.
- **Multilingual Localization:** Designed around Turkish, English, German, Spanish, and Bosnian language versions.
- **Visual Storytelling:** Atmospheric background visuals and sound effects contribute to the experience.
- **Cultural Adaptation:** Content is approached with attention to cultural context, natural language, and the preservation of mythological names.

## Localization Approach

The project follows a localization-oriented approach that considers more than direct translation. Its intended principles include:

- Using natural, audience-appropriate language in each target locale.
- Preserving the names of mythological figures.
- Considering cultural references and geographical context.
- Maintaining a clear, accessible, and informative storytelling style.
